## 1. Why a swim brand would buy this (the thesis)

These are gear-and-apparel companies, not software companies. They don't buy apps for the code — they buy them for **direct reach into swimmers and the athlete relationship/data that comes with it.** Their structural weakness: they sell *through* retail and clubs, so they don't own the direct relationship with the individual swimmer.

**Lead the pitch with the equipment hook.** The app's per-session equipment selection is a native, in-context surface to recommend *their* paddles, fins, snorkels, tech suits — to the exact 11–18 demographic they compete for. That reframes SwimFitPro from "a training tool" to **"a branded athlete channel + equipment funnel with a proprietary performance dataset."**

The moat narrative: the Performance Correlation Engine (and the other back-end engines) plus an accumulating dataset of *what training actually drives faster swims* — the "why not just build it ourselves" answer.

## 3. Known tech note (not a blocker)

The app is a single ~13,000-line HTML file. Fine for shipping and fine for an **asset deal**; it will get discounted in technical due diligence as "rebuild cost." **Do not let anyone talk you into a from-scratch rewrite before you have users.** Users first — architecture is a post-traction problem.

---

## 4. Realistic path (be clear-eyed)

At today's stage — pre-revenue, weak engagement, solo non-dev founder — a clean outright acquisition is a stretch. The sequence that actually works:

1. **Partnership / pilot / licensing first, not acquisition.** Get one brand to co-brand or white-label the app for their sponsored teams/clubs. Lower-risk "yes" for them — and it *creates* the acquisition case.
2. **Hit a traction milestone** (see below) so the audience is provable.
3. **Then** open the acquisition / acqui-hire conversation with leverage. Interest from *two* of the three is what actually moves the price.
